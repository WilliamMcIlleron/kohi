The name

Kōhī just means coffee in Japanese. The rest came later: the matcha, the records, and the jazz kissa habit of sitting still long enough to actually listen.

The room

Bare plaster, a lot of wood, and speakers that take up more room than they should.

Kōhī is a café first. It's also a small gallery, and a listening room whenever someone puts a record on. The turntables sit behind the counter, next to the coffee, which tells you most of what you need to know about the place.

The room borrows from Japan and from Joburg in roughly equal measure. Restraint and good joinery from one, jazu and the hospitality from the other.

Coffee

Beans we know the source of, roasted light enough to taste. Espresso, or filter if you've got the time.

Matcha

Whisked to order, hot or over ice. Plenty of people come in for this and never touch the coffee.

Pastry

Croissants, danishes, Basque cheesecake. Baked in the morning and usually gone by mid-afternoon.

Kōhī Sessions

Bring a record.

First and last Sunday of the month, the room turns into a listening session. Bring something to play if you've got it. If not, come anyway and sit at the back. Coffee and pastry run the whole time.
